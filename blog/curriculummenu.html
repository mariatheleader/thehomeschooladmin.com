Homeschool Curriculum Menu for Maine Families (2026)

If you've ever sat in front of a hundred open tabs trying to choose homeschool curriculum, this page is for you. This Curriculum Menu is a curated list of options by subject—so you can see trusted resources at a glance and build a plan that fits your child and Maine's requirements.

Use this as a reference page, a planning tool, and a place to come back to whenever you're adjusting curriculum mid‑year.

How to Use This Curriculum Menu

This menu is not a prescription—it's a starting point. Pair it with your child's Learning Languages (how they actually learn) and Maine's subject requirements. If you haven't read it yet, this post works beautifully alongside:

  • How to Start Homeschooling in Maine (2026 Guide)

For each subject below, you'll see:

  • Trusted curriculum and resource options
  • Ideas for local or real‑world learning
  • A planning prompt so you can decide what you'll actually use

Core Subjects

English & Language Arts

Reading, writing, spelling, grammar, and communication all live here. You can go fully structured, fully literature‑based, or somewhere in between.

  • Khan Academy
  • Time 4 Learning
  • IXL
  • Tree House School House
  • Gather Round
  • The Good & The Beautiful
  • Harbor & Sprout

Planning prompt: How does your child best engage with language—through stories, workbooks, read‑alouds, or projects? Choose 1–2 resources that match that.

Math

Math can be conceptual, hands‑on, visual, or mastery‑based. The right fit depends a lot on your child's Learning Languages and your comfort level teaching math.

  • Khan Academy
  • Time 4 Learning
  • IXL
  • Math‑U‑See
  • Gather Round
  • The Good & The Beautiful
  • Dad's Worksheets

Planning prompt: Does your child need more visual support, more repetition, or more real‑life application? Let that guide your choice.

Science

From nature‑based learning to lab‑style experiments, science is a great place to lean into curiosity and hands‑on exploration.

  • Khan Academy
  • Time 4 Learning
  • IXL
  • Tree House School House
  • Gather Round
  • The Good & The Beautiful
  • Harbor & Sprout
  • MEL Science

Planning prompt: Does your child light up with experiments, nature walks, or videos? Choose resources that match that energy.

Social Studies

History, geography, civics, and culture can be textbook‑based or story‑driven. Many families love using living books and projects here.

  • Khan Academy
  • Time 4 Learning
  • IXL
  • Gather Round
  • Homeschool in The Woods
  • Tuttle Twins
  • Harbor & Sprout

Planning prompt: What time periods or topics is your child naturally curious about? Start there and build outward.

Health & Physical Education

Health and PE don't have to look like a traditional gym class. They can be woven into your weekly rhythm in simple, sustainable ways.

  • The Homeschool Mom's List
  • Maine Homeschool Sports League
  • Local extracurriculars (sports, dance, martial arts, etc.)
  • Create an exercise routine
  • Find local trails to explore
  • The Good & The Beautiful (health resources)
  • Mindfulness practices
  • MEL Med (melscience.com)
  • First Aid lessons

Planning prompt: What movement and health habits do you want to normalize in your home this year?

Fine Arts

Fine arts are where creativity, beauty, and expression live. They also give kids a powerful outlet when academics feel heavy.

  • The Good & The Beautiful (art & music)
  • Tree House School House
  • Classics for Kids
  • Squilt Listening Calendar

Planning prompt: Do you want to focus more on visual art, music, or both? Choose one simple way to include it weekly.

Library Skills & Maine Studies

These two areas are especially fun for Maine families because they connect directly to your local community and state.

Library Skills

  • Library guide videos
  • A curriculum by grade level
  • Free lesson plans
  • "Visit a Library" series

Planning prompt: How often can you realistically visit the library? Build that into your routine on purpose.

Maine Studies

  • Maine Studies Workbook
  • Roots & Shoots community project

Planning prompt: What local places, people, or events could become part of your Maine Studies this year?

For more on Maine‑specific requirements, see How to Start Homeschooling in Maine (2026 Guide).

Computer Proficiency

Maine requires computer proficiency once between grades 7–12. You can meet this requirement with a mix of typing, digital literacy, and coding.

  • The Good & The Beautiful (computer resources)
  • Free Computer Skills for Kids
  • Free lessons at Code.org
  • Coding games at Tynker
  • Typing.Academy

Planning prompt: Do you want to focus more on typing, basic computer use, or coding this year?

Additional Options & Enrichment

Beyond core curriculum, enrichment can make your homeschool feel rich, connected, and joyful.

  • OUTSCHOOL – Live online courses for all kinds of subjects and interests
  • Maine Enrichment Experiences – A 5‑class series of socialization experiences offered to homeschooling families in creative, educational 1.25‑hour group lessons taught by The Homeschool Admin.

Maine Enrichment Experiences go beyond the topic to cultivate an appreciation for learning, build community among homeschool families, and showcase homeschoolers' hard work.
